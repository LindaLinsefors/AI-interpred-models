# Tape investigation, verifier of arm F_unit

## Summary

I rebuilt the mode projectors from the eigen-decomposition of A = W[o,o] myself (agreement with tape_common.P_TAPE to 4e-16), took states directly from run(x)[:, t] instead of the zero-prefix trick, and recomputed every strong or moderate claim with my own code and seeds; the investigator's scripts do what the claims say, their train/test splits are clean, and I found no tape definition that differs from tape_common.py. All headline numbers reproduce within 0.01 (tape piece alone ranks the runner-up first in 0.839 of quiet final states, full score 0.974; slope 71-73 against 74; same best subsets; same deletion table; same variance shares; same weights-level kernel). Four interpretations need correcting. (1) "The 7 tape coordinates do not contain the runner-up's position" is true only for linear decoders: boosted trees and a small MLP on the scale-free tape coordinates alone reach 0.949-0.954 (linear 0.902, the network's own readout pattern applied to the tape piece 0.841, full score 0.974), so the tape is nearly self-contained as information and not self-contained only as a linear readout. (2) "The value comparison is in the tape alone" fails for entries of age 2, where the transient mode holds 18-22 of the 71-73 logits of slope; it holds for ages >= 4. (3) The double dissociation tape/clock is real but weaker than deletion suggests: when a part is resampled from another quiet state, the tape changes 39-52% of answers in the class where the old maximum becomes the answer (deletion 30%) and the clock changes 18-41% in the class where the top two stay (deletion 7-9%); deleting level or runner also changes 70-91% there, so deletion alone is not specific. (4) The final-readout substitution test of the tape's contents is circular, as the investigator suspected: the fitted tape piece alone ranks the runner-up first in 0.88-0.93, above the true tape piece's 0.84, and it reproduces the network's wrong choices in only 3-12% of the states where the network errs. Two things the investigator left open I could settle: the top-three description of the tape survives a continued run (3.1-5.3% of final answers change when it replaces the tape part of the state at step 6 or 8, against 57-79% for deletion; donors with no fit give 12-13% for the same ages and 5-7% when S/M is also matched), and the slow lobe after a maximum's write, cancelled by level + runner + clock, is seen on data with gates held fixed for a maximum 3-6 steps old (not for 7-8). I did not write tape_v2.md (this step is verification only and subagents may not write .md files); the conclusions are in this return value for the synthesis step.

## Corrected answer

Terms. $M_t \ge S_t \ge T_t$ are the three largest inputs so far (maximum, runner-up, third), and $a_M, a_S, a_T$ are their ages (steps since each arrived). A quiet state has $a_M \ge 2$, $a_S \ge 2$, $S_t > 0$. An old entry is a score entry of age $a \ge 2$ (score index $9 - a$). The tape piece is $s^{\text{tape}} = U_o P_{\text{tape}} h[o]$, the rest piece is $s^{\text{rest}} = U h - s^{\text{tape}}$ (modes level, runner, clock, trans, echo, and the pulse units). "Ranks the runner-up first" means the runner-up's entry is the largest old entry. All numbers are for quiet states on $\mathcal N(0,1)$ inputs unless stated.

What survives, from this angle:

1. The tape holds which old input is the runner-up, and for every old input its comparison with the runner-up. Three independent supports. (a) If the tape piece of a random other quiet state (rescaled by the ratio of the maxima) is combined with a state's own rest piece, the top old entry is the donor's runner-up in 81% and the state's own in 3%; the reverse combination gives 81% for the state's own. (b) A nonlinear decoder recovers the runner-up's position from the 7 tape coordinates alone in 95% (whole state 97.4%). (c) Near the runner-up, $s^{\text{tape}}[9-a] - s^{\text{tape}}[9-a_S] \approx b\,(x_{t-a} - S_t) + \text{offset}$ with $b = 71$–$73$ logits per unit at $t = 9$ (63 over all steps, 75 on uniform inputs), against $b = 74$ for the full score. For entries of age $\ge 4$ the rest piece has slope $-1$, so the comparison is in the tape alone; for entries of age 2 the transient mode holds 18–22 of the 71–73, so the statement does not hold for the newest old entry.

2. The tape is not self-contained as a linear readout, but it is nearly self-contained as information. Read with the network's own pattern $U$, the tape piece alone ranks the runner-up first in 83.9% (full score 97.4%; all steps 90.0% against 97.7%), and no linear decoder I fitted exceeds 90%. A nonlinear decoder reaches 95%. What the tape piece lacks is not the slope but an offset: its comparison error is $0.12\,M$ (sd) against $0.039\,M$.

3. The rest piece supplies that offset. It is a pattern over the old entries that depends on $(a_M, a_S)$ jointly, on $S_t/M_t$, and on the two newest inputs $x_t, x_{t-1}$. Adding a fitted version of it to the true tape piece gives 91.6% (pattern per $(a_M, a_S)$ times $M$), 94.5% (linear in $M, S$), 95.8% (plus $x_t, x_{t-1}$), against 97.3% for the true rest. Without any fit, the rest piece of another state gives 81.3% (random state, worse than no rest at all, 83.9%), 88.3% (same $(a_M, a_S)$) and 92.2% (same $(a_M, a_S)$ and nearest $S/M$). No single mode does this: runner + clock + trans + echo are needed for 96.9%, and which one helps most changes with the configuration (trans when the runner-up is two steps old, clock when the runner-up arrived before the maximum, runner when it arrived after and is 3–4 steps old). The weights show why: a write is spread over all nine modes, and the tape's share contains a false lobe at the oldest entries that the transient mode cancels for about two steps (with a runner-up two steps old the tape piece fails in 50% of states, the age-9 entry is on top in 78% of those, and adding trans repairs 68%); the maximum's write leaves a lobe on the entries older than the maximum, cancelled by level + runner + clock when the maximum is 3–6 steps old.

4. As a function of named quantities: the tape part of the state is, to a functional precision of 3–5% of final answers, a function of $V = \{M, S, T, a_M, a_S, a_T\}$, linear in $(M, S, T)$ for fixed ages. Replacing the tape part of the state at step 6 or 8 by that function and continuing the run changes 3.1% and 4.4% of final answers (deleting it: 57% and 79%; the tape of a random quiet state: 53% and 77%). The ages alone are not enough: the tape of another state with the same three ages changes 12%, and 5–7% when $S/M$ is matched as well. In variance, $V$ without the third explains 70–78% of each tape mode's piece and $V$ explains 86–92%. The remaining ~10% is not negligible in one respect: in the states where the network's own top old entry is wrong, the description reproduces the wrong choice in only 3–12%, so the network's errors live in what the description leaves out. The four modes are not separately meaningful (replacing one by its description costs 0.5–2.5 points, replacing all four together costs nothing at the readout); the short clock carries 57% of the comparison slope at $t = 9$ and 48% over all steps.

5. Function: the tape is what the network needs to name the runner-up among old inputs, and the main clock is what it mostly needs to name the old maximum once it is overtaken. With the top two unchanged until the end, deleting the tape changes 93–96% of answers and replacing it by another quiet state's tape 72–86%; for the clock 7–9% and 18–41%. When the old maximum is later overtaken and becomes the answer: tape 30% and 39–52%, clock 88–90% and 75–79%. This is a difference of degree, about 2:1 each way under resampling, not a clean separation, and deletion alone is not specific (deleting level or runner also changes 70–91%).

Not established: why the offset depends on both ages jointly; what the last 10% of each tape mode is (it carries the errors); whether $S/M$, $a_M$, $a_T$ and $T/M$ can each be decoded from the 7 coordinates (I tested only the runner-up's position); why a foreign tape changes 39–52% of answers when the old maximum should be named. Values divided by $M$ have heavy tails, so variance shares of single entries move by several points between seeds (0.54–0.60 and 0.70–0.78 for the first two shares of the rest piece); the functional numbers are stable to 0.005.
