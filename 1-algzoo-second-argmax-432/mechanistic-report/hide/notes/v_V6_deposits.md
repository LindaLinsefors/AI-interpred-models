# Verification bundle V6_deposits

## Summary

All four claims reproduce numerically with my own code; D1, D3 and D4 are CONFIRMED, D2 is CORRECTED because its numbers only match if the g ≥ 1 condition is dropped. The interpretation is half right: the first-harmonic clock bump is real, but "lead ∝ (M0 − S0)(1 − cos(42.75°·separation))" is incomplete, and the step to "errors at separation 7 or 8" is wrong for 8 and holds for only two configurations at 7.

Data: 4M sequences (seeds 5000..5009, 10 × 400k) for D1–D4; controlled sampling with seed 5100 (100k base sequences placed in each of the 720 configurations (pM, pS, pT)); seeds 5200..5203 for the clock intervention, 5300 for input traces, 5020 and 5030 for small checks. I opened only the lead's e14_score_updates.py, after my own numbers existed, to explain the D2 discrepancy. Tolerance applied: 5% relative, or sampling noise of the lead's 600k-sequence sample.

**What survives (part 1, the bump)**
- **Causal test.** Scaling the 42.75° clock coordinate of h_(t−1) by f = 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5 gives cosine-law amplitude K = −7.8, 27.6, 57.6, 73.2. Scaling the 83°, 125° or alternating modes moves K by under 3%.
- **Regression-free check.** The old max's exact input-trace footprint over the tape is a harmonic with best common frequency 43.0° (SSE within 10% of the minimum for 40.75°–45.25°; pooled R² 0.905).
- **Before and after.** The old runner-up leads before the record in 99.5% of D1 records; the old max leads after in 97.2%.

**What needs correcting (part 1, the law)**
- The coefficient c1 is a function of the two ages at the record, aM = t − p_M0 and aS = t − p_S0, not of the separation alone. It does not depend on t (spread ≤ 4.0).
- Pooled centred R² of y over all records: 0.754 for the cosine law, 0.761 for any function of Δ, 0.881 for signed separation, 0.964 for the pair of ages.
- The law holds when the old runner-up is at least two steps old (aS ≥ 2, 76% of records): c1/(1 − cos) = 45.0, 52.9, 54.2, 54.9, 53.1, 51.7, 49.7 for Δ = 1..7, R² = 0.997 over that table.
- Its amplitude depends on order: K = 65.7 when the old runner-up is older than the old max, 42.5 when younger.
- It fails when the old runner-up arrived on the step before the record (aS = 1, 24% of records): c1 = 34.5, 32.9, 39.3, 41.8, 51.1, 63.6, 55.9, 54.6 for Δ = 1..8, with no cosine shape. This produces the lead's outliers 65 and 82.
- Δ = 8 was dropped from the lead's table: c1 = 54.6 where the law gives 2.2 (ratio 1116), P(y > 0) = 0.994.

**What fails (part 2, "consequently 7 or 8")**
- **D = 8.** 96–99% of the "answers the third" errors (bins 1–3) come from (pS, pT, pM) = (8, 0, 9), a g = 0 configuration that D1 excludes. The only D1-type configuration, (0, 8, 9), has the lowest error rates in the table: 0.034, 0.010, 0.005, 0.001.
- **D = 7.** Configurations (0, 7, 9) and (7, 0, 9) carry 42%, 86%, 99%, 97% of the errors per bin. There c1 = 21.6 and 29.3, matching the law's 23.0, with negative offsets c2 = −3.98 and −1.68. The other four D = 7 configurations have at most 1.2% errors for gaps above 8%.
- **Late max confound.** Records on the last step (pM = 9, 30% of configurations) carry 60–81% of all errors in bins 2–4.
- **Variance explained.** Separation explains 8–19% of the between-configuration variance of the error rate; the pair of ages explains 57–94%.
- **Max-first control.** No excess at D = 7–8 when the max comes first.
- **Error type.** Errors at D = 7–8 are specifically "answers the third" (79–90% of errors in bins 1–3).

**Side findings**
- D2's "decay" from 40.7 to 23.8 is mostly a composition effect: fixed cohorts end at 89–109% of their k = 0 value.
- D3's k1 depends on the two ages, not on t.
