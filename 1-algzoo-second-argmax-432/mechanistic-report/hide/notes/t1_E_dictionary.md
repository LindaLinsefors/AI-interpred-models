# Tape investigation, arm E_dictionary

## Answer

Method and terms. I built controlled sequences (maximum $M = 1$, runner-up $S$, one or two further inputs, every other input a "filler": 0, $-1$, or draws of $-|N(0,1)|$) and call the "write" of an input the change of the 7 tape coordinates when that input replaces the filler. I write the tape as complex coordinates $z_m$, $m \in$ (alt, short, h2, h3), with eigenvalues $\lambda_m$. A "candidate" is an input that, when it arrived, was among the two largest so far or above 0.75 of the runner-up of that moment.

What is established. (1) One clean dictionary entry exists: an input $x_p < S$ that arrives after both the maximum and the runner-up writes, at age $a \ge 2$, $\Delta z_m = A\,k_m\,\lambda_m^{a-2}$ with one fixed pattern $k = (1,\ 0.65e^{-i140^\circ},\ 0.79e^{-i81^\circ},\ 0.87e^{-i51^\circ})$ (the pattern that unit 5's pulse writes; 97.5% of the squared norm) and amplitude $A = g\,(x_p - \Theta)_+$, $g = 19.1$, $\Theta \approx 0.75\,S$ in the median. So for such an input the tape holds its age (as the factor $\lambda_m^{a}$) and its excess over about three quarters of the runner-up (as the amplitude); below $\Theta$ only a linear leak $(\ell_m^\top w[o])\,x_p\,\lambda_m^{a}$ with coefficients 0.17 to 0.31 is written. (2) $\Theta$ is not a fixed multiple of $S$: it ranges over 0.39 to 0.82 $S$ with the ages of maximum and runner-up at arrival (down to 0.07 $S$ in corner configurations), and every write raises it for later inputs, $\Theta \to \Theta + 0.41\,(x_p - \Theta)_+$. Hence two written inputs are not additive (the deviation is 0.6 to 0.9 of the smaller single write). (3) These writes are a small part of real tapes: in random quiet final states they carry 3% of the tape's variance. The bulk comes from the maximum, the runner-up and "dethroned" inputs (in the top two at arrival, no longer so), and for these the form amplitude times fixed pattern times $\lambda^{a}$ fails: a lone maximum's writes cancel from $19.8\,M$ at age 1 to $3.0\,M$ at age 2 and the remainder does not follow the eigenvalues until age 6; the tape of a pair $(M, S)$ differs from the sum of the single tapes by 6 to 17 times the single runner-up tape; and with the real pulses of units 4, 5, 9 as amplitudes and fixed patterns the tape of random quiet states is reproduced with $R^2 = 0.23$ (58% of answers change). (4) What the tape depends on, measured by replacing it with the tape of a skeleton sequence (named inputs kept, the rest set to 0): values and ages of $M, S$ give $R^2 = 0.56$ (7.3% of answers change), top three 0.85 (6.5%), top four 0.95 (5.4%), the candidates (4.8 inputs per state) 0.974 (4.9%; 2.8% when the linear leak of the dropped inputs is added), against 91% changed answers for the mean tape. Inputs that arrived between the maximum and the runner-up matter most beyond the top two (0.56 to 0.85), inputs after both hardly (0.56 to 0.58).

So from this angle: the tape stores the values and ages of the inputs that were top-two candidates when they arrived (about five numbers-pairs, more than its 7 coordinates can hold independently), encoded as a sum of age-rotated writes only for late near-runner-up inputs, and as a strongly non-additive, order-dependent function for the top two and the dethroned inputs.

Not established: a closed formula for the top-two and dethroned part (I could only evaluate it by running the skeleton); my explicit formula for the later writes reproduces 59 to 77% of their contribution but does not pass the functional test. Capacity was measured only for formula-generated tapes: the 7 coordinates determine amplitudes on the 8 age slots up to one null direction (condition number 6.1), and up to 3 writes are read back in 92% of cases at a noise of 0.3 per coordinate. I did not write tape_v2.md (investigators return text only); these findings are input for it.

## Did not work

- A generative formula that is a sum over written inputs with amplitudes given by named quantities of the final state: not possible in that form, because writes depend on rank and thresholds at arrival and are not additive (deviation 0.6-0.9 of a single write for two late inputs, 6-17 for maximum plus runner-up).
- The tape as real pulse amplitudes of units 4, 5, 9 times fixed patterns times eigenvalue^age: R2 0.23 at t = 9 and -0.61 at t = 6, 58% and 36% of answers changed.
- Formula N1 (skeleton (M, S) plus explicit later writes with threshold 0.75 S): better R2 in the 13% of states it touches, but more answers changed than the skeleton (M, S) alone (13.7% against 11.3% in states with one later write).
- A single 'maximum pattern': the write of a maximum that overtakes an earlier one leaves 72-100% of its norm off the lone maximum's pattern, and the lone maximum's tape does not follow the eigenvalues before age 6.
- A threshold that is a fixed multiple of the runner-up: Theta/S ranges from 0.07 to 0.83 over the 42 arrival configurations and shifts with S/M and with negative fillers.
- Decoding real tapes with the dictionary's pseudo-inverse: the largest slot is the runner-up's in 74%, worse than the network's own tape readout (84%).

## Open

- A closed form for the dominant part of the tape, the top two and the dethroned inputs, as a function of S/M, the two ages, the order of arrival and the dethroned values; I evaluated it only by running the skeleton sequence.
- What sets the write threshold Theta exactly (probably the runner coordinate feeding unit 5; not tested) and why it sags in the corner configurations (old runner-up with fresh maximum, old maximum with fresh runner-up).
- Whether a configuration-dependent threshold makes the explicit later-write formula pass the functional test.
- The remaining 2.8% of changed answers for the candidate skeleton with linear leak at t = 9: which dropped inputs or which interaction causes them.
- Capacity of real tapes: how many of the roughly five candidate values and ages are actually recoverable from 7 coordinates (the decoding investigator's angle).
- The threshold-raising law and the skeleton tests used filler 0 only; random low fillers were used only for the single-input entry and the additivity table.
- tape_v2.md was not written by this investigator (the step's rules allow no .md files and only the own folder); these findings are meant as input for it.
