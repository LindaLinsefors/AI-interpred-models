## 1. Bottom line

1. **The algorithm claimed.** At each step five comparators compare $x_t$ with thresholds that are linear readings of the state and sit near the maximum and runner-up so far. One step later the recorder $n_5$ writes a "record" of inputs near the top two. The 13 units that are on in the resting gate pattern carry each record through a different set of units at each age, and each readout row reads the record of the matching age. The maximum is kept from winning by a negative effect on its own logit: $n_4$ at arrival, then the timers $n_9$, $n_0$ and the comparators $n_1, n_6, n_7, n_8$, until a new maximum switches those comparators off ("relabeling").
2. **Evidence.** Weights, linear maps of measured modal gate patterns, sample statistics, per-cell fits and gate-forcing interventions. No decomposition components.
3. **Reproducibility.** Every weight-derived number I checked matches the checkpoint, and every measured number I re-measured matches (§9.1). The paper's numbers can be trusted; the open questions are about interpretation.
4. **What the paper says it has not derived:** which gate patterns occur, what the timers do to the maximum's trace, the recorder's coefficient on the runner-up so far, the comparators' gains on the runner-up, why the coefficients of the runner-up and the third largest come out opposite, the function of the switching of $n_3, n_{10}, n_{11}, n_{15}$, and the error rate itself (§8).
5. **My main flags (§9).** (a) The paper's wording for $n_0$ ("off, because $n_7$ and $n_9$ were both on") is a gate rule rather than the cause: with $n_9$ forced off, $n_0$ is still off at 71% of those steps, and with the maximum's own record in $n_5$ removed it is never off. (b) "Rotating tape" is loose for records: by the paper's own eigen-split a record rides three complex pairs with different speeds and decay rates and hardly touches the long-lived $0.95\angle 43°$ pair. (c) Per-cell threshold fits use an intercept in a bias-free network. (d) Negative inputs are barely covered. (e) The paper never discusses an offset or clipping, but its numbers contain the ingredients of your off-centre-circle idea (§9.4).

## 2. Setup, notation and definitions (p1–3, p6, p15)

**Model** (p1): $h_t = \mathrm{ReLU}(w\,x_t + W_{hh} h_{t-1})$, $h_{-1} = 0$, logits $= W_{oh} h_9$; 432 parameters in $w$ (16), $W_{hh}$ ($16\times16$) and $W_{oh}$ ($10\times16$). "the input $x_t$ at step $t$ sits at position $t$".

**Checkpoint and samples** (p15): ARC's `example_2nd_argmax()` checkpoint (sha256 `498bd9a8…18bb`), loaded into a JAX port of `alg_zoo`'s `DistRNN` "that matches ARC's torch forward pass to within $2\times10^{-4}$ in every logit". "Every analysis runs the network in float64 NumPy on fresh sequences of ten independent standard Gaussian inputs; the model's accuracy is 95.3% on $2^{23}$ of them." It errs on 4.7% of sequences. ✓S (95.32%)

**ARC's prior description** (p1) **[ARC]**: $n_2$ approximately holds the maximum of the inputs before the latest one; $n_4$ the increase in the running maximum; $n_1$, $n_6$, $n_7$ the maximum of some earlier inputs minus the latest one. A hand-built 22-neuron network computing all ten leave-one-out maxima reaches 99%. "The trained network's other eleven neurons are densely connected, and ARC could not match most of its neurons to hand-built ones."

**Method premise** (p1): "A ReLU network without biases is linear once it is known which neurons are on, so for each part of the answer we ask what switches the neurons and what the resulting linear maps do."

**Definitions** (p2–3 unless noted). "We ... report medians except where parts must add up, which we report as means."

## 3. The account compressed into equations

These are the paper's statements, in its numbers; evidence and pages are in §5.

1. **Compare** (step $t$). $\theta_j = -W_{hh}[j]\cdot h_{t-1}/w_j$ for $j\in\{4,7,6,1,8\}$; within a cell $\theta_j \approx g_{\max}x^{<t}_{\max} + g_{2\mathrm{nd}}x^{<t}_{2\mathrm{nd}} + b$. Activations: $h_t[4] = w_4\,(x_t-\theta_4)^+$ with $w_4 = +10.16$, and $h_t[j] = \lvert w_j\rvert\,(\theta_j - x_t)^+$ for $j = 7, 6, 1, 8$ with $\lvert w_j\rvert = 13.17, 11.00, 10.56, 12.31$.
2. **Record** (step $t+1$), medians:
   - new maximum that fired $n_4$: $h_{t+1}[5] \approx 12.0\,x_t - 0.9\,x^{<t}_{\max} - 0.3\,x^{<t}_{2\mathrm{nd}}$;
   - new runner-up that passed $n_8$'s threshold but not $n_7$'s: $h_{t+1}[5] \approx 7.7\,x_t - 2.0\,x^{<t}_{2\mathrm{nd}} + 0.8\,x^{<t}_{\max}$;
   - input that passed no threshold: $h_{t+1}[5] \approx (18.0\,x_t - 12.8\,x^{<t}_{2\mathrm{nd}})^+$, so it is recorded when $x_t \gtrsim 0.7\,x^{<t}_{2\mathrm{nd}}$ and otherwise "drops out of the state".
3. **Carry.** With the resting pattern the record's trace $r = 18.0\,e_5$ becomes $A^{k} r$ after $k$ more steps, moving through $n_0$ (age 2), $n_3, n_{12}, n_{13}$ (age 3), $n_{12}$ (age 4), $n_{11}$ (age 5), $n_{10}, n_{15}$ (age 6).
4. **Read.** Final logits per unit of an input recorded at position $p$: $W_{oh}A^{8-p}r$, which is $+62$ to $+76$ at position $p$, about $+20$ at $p+1$, and less or negative elsewhere. The last input has no record step; its logit is read from the comparators: $W_{oh}[9,7]\cdot 13.17\,(\theta_7 - x_9)$ with $W_{oh}[9,7] = -3.79$.
5. **Keep the maximum from winning.** Its own effect is $w_4 W_{oh}[9,4] = 10.16\times(-4.99) = -50.7$ at arrival, $-28$ one step later and about $-6$ at ages 2–4 (measured medians $-4$ to $-8$ at ages 2–9), until a new maximum switches $n_1, n_6, n_7, n_8$ off and the old maximum's own effect becomes $+30$.
6. **Decide.** Three margins, each exactly $\sum_p c_p x_p$: against the maximum (median $+12$ logits, from the maximum's negative own effect), against the other inputs (few are recorded, later records pull earlier logits down), and against the third largest ($\approx 67$–$91\times(x_{2\mathrm{nd}} - x_{3\mathrm{rd}})$ on near ties, because the later of the two is recorded relative to the earlier).

## 5. Claims by mechanism

### 5.1 Comparators and their thresholds (Sections 2 and 9, Appendix A)

- **C1. Five neurons with large input weights switch at a threshold read linearly from the state.** $\theta_j = -W_{hh}[j]\cdot h_{t-1}/w_j$. $n_4$, "the only comparator with a positive input weight, is on when $x_t > \theta_4$"; the other four "are on when $x_t < \theta_j$, so passing their thresholds turns them off; when on, their activations $\lvert w_j\rvert(\theta_j - x_t)$ hold the input's distance below their thresholds". ### 5.8 How errors arise (Section 10, Appendix A)

- **E1. Size and kind.** "The model errs on 4.7% of sequences: it answers the third largest on 2.97%, the maximum on 0.95% and the fourth-largest or a smaller input on 0.76%." **[M]** p12. ✓S (2.95%, 0.94%, 0.78%)
- **E2. By gap.** "It answers the third largest on 38% of sequences in which the third differs from the runner-up by less than 0.01, on 7% at 0.04–0.05 and on 1.4% at 0.1–0.15, and it answers the maximum on 27% of those in which the maximum and the runner-up differ by less than 0.01." **[M]** $2^{22}$. p12.
- **E3. Shares of all errors** ($2^{21}$; Table 4 in §6). 48.4% answer the third largest on near ties with the runner-up, 7.9% at gaps of 0.05–0.1 and 7.1% at larger gaps. Errors that answer the maximum on near ties are 6.6% of all errors when the runner-up arrives first, 7.7% after a misfire at the runner-up's arrival and 0.6% with a later runner-up and no misfire; 5.4% answer the maximum away from near ties. Of the errors that answer the fourth-largest or a smaller input, 1.6% of all errors are within 0.05 of the runner-up and 14.7% further. Figure 11a: "65% within 0.05". **[M]** p12, p18. ✓S (48.3%, 14.8%, 1.6%, 35.2%)
- **E4. Near ties with the third: the "remainder" outweighs the comparison.** "We fix the comparison between the runner-up and the third largest at $61\,(x_{2\mathrm{nd}} - x_{3\mathrm{rd}})$, with 61 the median over all sequences of $(c_{2\mathrm{nd}} - c_{3\mathrm{rd}})/2$" ($g = 61.0$, p18), "lower than the 67–91 of the near ties in Section 8 (means)":
  $$\text{margin} = 61\,(x_{2\mathrm{nd}} - x_{3\mathrm{rd}}) + \underbrace{(c_{2\mathrm{nd}} - 61)\,x_{2\mathrm{nd}} + (c_{3\mathrm{rd}} + 61)\,x_{3\mathrm{rd}} + (\text{the maximum's term}) + (\text{the other inputs' terms})}_{\text{remainder}} .$$
  "Of the remainder's variance on near ties, 43% comes from the two values' departures from $\pm61$, 40% from the maximum's term and 17% from the other inputs" (variance shares are $\mathrm{cov}(\text{part}, \text{remainder})/\mathrm{var}(\text{remainder})$). **[M]** p12–13, p18.
- **E5. A gap-independent remainder predicts the error rate only between 0.01 and 0.075.** "The third's logit exceeds the runner-up's exactly when the remainder is below $-61$ times the gap, so the rate at each gap $d$ would be the share of near-tie remainders below $-61d$. This fits the observed rate to within 1 percentage point at gaps from 0.01 to 0.075, though only 0.05–0.075 lies outside the near ties the remainders come from. It underestimates the rate below 0.01 (34% against 40%; the model answers the third largest on 38% there, since the maximum's logit can exceed both) and above 0.075." Observed against implied by bin: 0.395 against 0.335 below 0.01, 0.244 against 0.244 at 0.01–0.02, 0.079 against 0.086 at 0.04–0.05, 0.033 against 0.021 at 0.075–0.1 and 0.019 against 0.007 at 0.1–0.15. **[M]** $2^{21}$. p13, p18–19, Fig 11b.
- **E6. A quarter of the third-largest near-tie errors come from records the network need not make** (N8). **[I]** p12–13.
- **E7. Misfires cost a modest share.** "Forcing $n_4$ off at the runner-up's arrival lowers the 14% of these near ties on which the model then answers the maximum (Section 6) to 6%. ### 5.9 Rules for every gate (Appendix B, p19–20)

- **G1. The rule network.** $h_t = G_t\,(w\,x_t + W_{hh}h_{t-1})$ with each gate in $G_t$ set by an explicit rule from step 2 on; steps 0 and 1 keep the model's gates. Rules fitted on $2^{18}$ sequences and tested on $2^{18}$ others, on which the model's accuracy is 95.4%. "errors reproduced" is "the fraction of the model's errors on which the network gives the same wrong answer".
- **G2. Comparators.** "For each neuron and step, the threshold rule is whichever of two forms fits the threshold better on the fit set: linear in $(x^{<t}_{\max}, x^{<t}_{2\mathrm{nd}}, 1)$ per cell; or linear in the individual stored values, each placed in a slot indexed by its age, its rank among the inputs so far (maximum, runner-up, third, other) and what happened when it arrived (new maximum, entered the top two, or below the runner-up), plus the gates of $n_1, n_4, n_5, n_6, n_7, n_8$ and $n_9$ at the two previous steps, alone and multiplied by the maximum and the runner-up so far. The current input is not a feature. The slot form wins at early steps, the cell form from about step 5."
- **G3. Recorder, timers, memory.** "Neuron $n_9$ copies $n_4$'s gate, and $n_0$ is off when $n_7$ and $n_9$ were both on, at the previous step. The recorder $n_5$'s pre-activation is fitted per (step, cell of the previous step) on $x_t$, on how far the previous input passed each comparator's rule threshold, that is, on $\max(x_{t-1}-\theta_4, 0)$ and $\max(\theta_j - x_{t-1}, 0)$ for $j = 1, 6, 7, 8$, and on the maximum and the runner-up so far and 1; at step 2, $n_5$ is off when $n_1$, $n_6$ and $n_8$ were all on at step 1." "Neurons $n_2, n_{12}, n_{13}$ and $n_{14}$ are on from step 1. For $n_3, n_{10}, n_{11}$ and $n_{15}$, the pre-activation is, per neuron and step, the better of a fit linear in the four largest inputs so far, $x_t$, the mean of the rest and a constant, per cell with at least 40 sequences, and a fit linear in the slot values of $x_0,\dots,x_t$ plus the previous gates of the comparators, the recorder and the timers, alone and multiplied by $x_t$ and the maximum and the runner-up so far."
- **G4. Result.** Tables 5 and 6 (§6). "The rule network reproduces 67% of the model's errors and 96.6% of its correct answers. The model's own gates with small random noise, 0.015 standard deviations of each neuron's pre-activation added before its gate is decided, reproduce more of its errors at the same accuracy (85%): noise flips only gates whose pre-activations are near zero, whereas the rules' threshold errors of about 0.1 change which sequences fail." Baseline: the exact second argmax of inputs perturbed by Gaussian noise "of standard deviation 0.021 to match the model's accuracy or 0.032 to match the rule network's". "holding $n_2, n_{12}, n_{13}$ and $n_{14}$ on costs only 0.7 points of decisions but 7 points of reproduced errors; and the early-step slot rules add 1.2 points of decisions over cell rules alone." "The rules describe the gates; they do not compress them. They have 16,827 coefficients, forty times the network's 432 parameters." **[F]**, **[I]**.

## 8. Stated uncertainties, failed attempts and open questions (the paper's own)

1. **Status.** "Draft." "This is a mechanistic description, not the mechanistic estimate of accuracy that ARC takes as the standard for an explanation." (p1) "Much of it follows from the weights, but which neurons are on is described by measurements and fits, and the error rates are measured." (p2)
2. **Lead over the third not derived.** "On average this makes the runner-up's lead over the third proportional to the gap between their values; we measure this but do not derive it." (p1) "that they balance is measured, and we see the three parts but have not derived why they add up to opposite coefficients." (p10)
3. **Recorder's coefficient on the runner-up.** "the 18 follows from the weights, and the 12.8 is a near-tie median" (p4). "the memory terms cancel the maximum's term from the thresholds and supply about half of the runner-up's coefficient (Appendix A); we have not split them by neuron." (p5)
4. **Record values only roughly captured.** "The formulas predict when the recorder turns on, but only roughly what it records. Put in place of $n_5$ at the steps they cover, they keep 98% of the model's decisions when they set only whether $n_5$ is on, but 78% when they also set its value" (p5). Discussion: "not all of what it records (78–81%)".
5. **The resting map is partial.** "The resting pattern covers only part of the steps" (21%, 51% with one-flip variants). "Older records fade faster than the resting map predicts: $+55$ and $+39$ at positions 3 and 2, against $+76$ and $+72$ derived." (p5)
6. **Derived own effect of the maximum.** Good only "through age 4"; "$+0.4$ at age 9, against $-3.7$ measured"; "five groups are off by 12–23" (p6); correlation 0.75 over the 45 groups in which the runner-up arrives later (p18).
7. **Timers.** "The timers' part is near zero only pooled over ages ($-38$ to $+37$ by age; Appendix A); what they do to the trace in its first two steps does not show in this split." (Fig 5 caption) Discussion lists "what the timers do to the maximum's trace" as measured.
8. **Gains on the runner-up.** Derivation from the six commonest arrival patterns has "errors up to 0.4"; they "cover 37% of arrivals" (p16).
9. **Failed attempt: undoing the drift.** "Undoing the drift by raising $n_4$'s threshold, however, makes $n_4$ miss new maxima and lowers the accuracy by 0.2 points" (p11). Details (p19): raising $\theta_4$ by $(\rho(1)-\rho(a))\,x^{<t}_{\max}$ "cuts the misfires on near ties at ages 4–9 to 40–44%, but $n_4$ then misses the maximum's arrival on 0.8% of sequences, against 0.1% in the model"; "The threshold's spread on near ties grows with age as much as its median falls ..., so a threshold raised near the maximum puts the upper part of its spread above new maxima."
10. **Remainder model.** Holds "from 0.01 to 0.075 but not at larger gaps"; "only 0.05–0.075 lies outside the near ties the remainders come from"; underestimates below 0.01 and above 0.075 (p13).
11. **Too few to analyse.** Errors that "answer a smaller input within 0.05 of the runner-up" (2% of errors; Table 4).
12. **The rules do not compress.** "The rules are approximate." "The rules describe the gates; they do not compress them. They have 16,827 coefficients, forty times the network's 432 parameters." The model's own gates with small noise reproduce more errors (85%) than the rule network (67%) at the same accuracy (p20).
13. **Memory neurons without a function.** "Four memory neurons, $n_3$, $n_{10}$, $n_{11}$ and $n_{15}$, switch by fitted rules, and we have not given their switching a function." (p14)
14. **What follows from the weights and what is measured** (p14). "Four parts of this account follow from the weights: that an input passing no threshold reaches only the recorder, with a coefficient of 18; how the resting map carries a record and where the readout reads it; the comparators' gains on the maximum, which largely set where their thresholds sit as the maximum ages; the maximum's own effect through age 4, given the commonest gate pattern at each step." "The rest is measured: which gate patterns occur, what the timers do to the maximum's trace, the recorder's coefficient on the runner-up so far, the records and readouts of the runner-up's own routes, the comparators' gains on the runner-up, the balance that makes the coefficients of the runner-up and the third largest opposite, and the error rates."
15. **Not an explanation by ARC's standard** (p14). "By ARC's standard this is not yet an explanation. ... We have no such estimate. Our account suggests a route: write the error rate as a sum over the orders of arrival and the record and misfire cases. Each term is an integral over Gaussian inputs of a margin that is linear wherever the gates are fixed. What remains is to derive those regions and margins from the weights instead of measuring them, and to make the records' values exact."
16. **Samples differ between statements.** "Figure 7 uses another sample" (p16); "The splits by neuron group below are also means, from a separate run on another sample, so they differ slightly from the second table" (p17).

## 9. My assessment (mine, not the paper's)

### 9.1 What I reproduced

Everything I tried reproduces; I found no wrong number.

- **From the checkpoint (✓W):** all 16 input weights of Table 1; the six quoted recurrent weights (0.969, 1.731, 0.595, 1.187, $-0.593$, $-0.850$) and the two quoted readout weights ($-4.992$, $-3.789$); the routing vector $W_{hh}D_tw$ in both patterns and the "7.5–18.2 on $n_5$, up to 19.5 elsewhere" range; the eigenvalues of $A$; the derived readout of Figure 4a; the carrier neurons at ages 2–6; the eigen-split of a record's own effect; the derived gains on the maximum and the length 40.7; the step-1 threshold of $n_4$; the $-6$ / $+7$ of the $n_0$ relay; and the example (all gates of Figure 2b, the three records, the final logits and the contributions of Figure 9, from inputs rounded to two decimals).
- **By simulation, $2^{19}$ sequences, seed 0 (✓S):** accuracy and the three error rates; Table 2 (44 of 45 cells identical); the pass rates; the gate statistics of $n_9$, $n_5$, $n_0$; the resting-pattern coverage (21% and 51%); the record rates by step; the 94%, 7.8%, 97%, 85% and 80% of the 0.7 rule; Table 3 (shares, recorded, the four trace columns and accuracy); 42% / 36% / 22%; the misfire rates by age and the drift of $\theta_4$; the error shares of Table 4; the maximum's own effect by age (medians identical to 0.1 at every age); the measured diagonal of Figure 4b; the five comparator knock-outs; the $n_5$ knock-out (63%) and the oracle (99.0%, 95.9%); the $n_9$ and $n_0$ interventions (67% with 87%, 43% with 65%); and the global misfire intervention ($+0.35$ points, fixes 0.44%, breaks 0.09%).
- **A tension I checked and resolved.** The targeted misfire intervention removes more maximum-errors than the global one seems to (14% to 6% on 2.5% of sequences, against "15% of the errors that answer the maximum"). Both are right: the global intervention also creates new maximum-errors on 0.10% of sequences.
- **Agreement with existing project findings.** The eigenvalues of $A$ are the rotation modes already in the project notes. Table 2 and the drift agree with the earlier finding that $-W_{hh}[4,\cdot]\,h_t/10.16$ sags after a new maximum, and `rotate/false_new_max.png` is a misfire in this paper's terms.

### 9.2 Inconsistencies inside the paper (all minor)

1. **The recorder's coefficient on the runner-up so far is quoted as four different things:** "13" (abstract, Figure 1), "12.8" (Section 3, "a near-tie median"), "8–12" (Section 8, means) and "9 to 14" (Appendix A, medians). The first appendix table gives $-12.8$, $-13.6$, $-11.9$, $-9.0$ by order. So 12.8 is the median of one order (max–2nd–3rd) on near ties only, and "0.7" $= 12.8/18$ inherits that. The directly measured half-point is 0.72–0.77 and depends on the maximum too.
2. **Two wordings for $n_9$'s gate.** Table 1 says "on one step after $n_7$ and $n_8$ were off"; the text and Appendix B say it copies $n_4$'s gate. I tested both: they agree with $n_9$'s gate on 98.99% and 98.98% of steps, so this is harmless.
3. **Sample-to-sample differences** between statements of the same quantity: the model answers the runner-up on 81.7% / 88.3% of near ties in one paragraph and 81.5% / 87.9% in another (p17, p19); the earlier value's share through $n_7$ is $-1.5$ on p15 and $-1.4$ on p18. All within noise.
4. **"Nearly always on" is loose for $n_{14}$.** On my sample $n_{14}$ is off on 9.9% of steps $\ge 1$ (36% of the steps at which $n_4$ is on, 3.2% of the others), about as often as $n_{10}$ (7.2%) and $n_{11}$ (8.4%), which the paper puts in the other group ("thresholds of their own"). For comparison $n_2$ 5.9%, $n_{12}$ 5.3%, $n_{13}$ 5.1%, $n_3$ 20.4%, $n_{15}$ 24.7%, $n_0$ 22.0%. With $w_{14} = -1.32$, $n_{14}$ behaves like a weak comparator at new maxima.

### 9.3 Claims that are under-supported or easy to over-read

1. **"$n_0$ is off, because $n_7$ and $n_9$ were both on" (X7b) is a gate rule, not the cause.** The rule matches 97.4% of steps, but I tested the causal claim directly ($2^{19}$ sequences). Two steps after a new maximum $n_0$ is off at 99.8% of steps. With $n_9$ forced off at the step after $n_4$ fires, $n_0$ is still off at 71.1%. With $n_5$ forced off at that step instead (the maximum's own record removed), $n_0$ is off at 0.0%. Mean terms $W_{hh}[0,j]\,h_{t-1}[j]$ of $n_0$'s pre-activation there: $-16.0$ from $n_7$, $-12.8$ from $n_5$ (through $W_{hh}[0,5] = -0.91$), $-8.4$ from $n_8$, $-2.9$ from $n_9$, $+10.2$ from $n_6$, sum $-4.0$. One step after an ordinary record the same terms are $-13.5$, $-4.6$, $-10.0$, $0.0$, $+16.4$, sum $+9.3$, and $n_0$ is off at 0.4% of steps. So $n_0$ is clipped mainly because the record of a new maximum ($\approx12\,x_{\max}$) is larger than $n_0$'s positive baseline can absorb; $n_9$ adds a small push. The paper's consequence still holds (the maximum's record is not relayed), but the trigger is the record's size. Forcing $n_5$ off after $n_4$ fires keeps 47.1% of decisions; the paper lists this test (p15) but reports no result for it.
2. **"Rotating tape" and "direction encodes its age" are loose for records.** By the paper's own eigen-split, a record's own effect at ages 2–6 is carried by three complex pairs with different angular speeds (83°, 125° and 43° per step) and decay rates (0.94, 0.84, 0.74), and "the pair of modulus 0.95 contributes less than 1 per unit". So a record does not ride the long-lived $0.95\angle43°$ clock. It is closer to a relay through age-specific carrier units ($n_0$, then $n_3, n_{12}, n_{13}$, then $n_{12}$, $n_{11}$, $n_{10}, n_{15}$). The paper does not say how a flat own effect of $+62$ to $+76$ over ages 1–7 comes out of modes that decay at different rates, and it gives no decoding or causal test that direction encodes age. The evidence is Figure 4a against 4b and trace cosines of 0.93–0.99.
3. **The paper never decomposes the maximum's trace by mode,** although the "thresholds move as the maximum's trace rotates" claim rests on it. See §9.4 for my split.
4. **"$n_2$ integrates the maximum" rests on two weights and ARC's statement.** It is right as far as I can tell: on my sample the correlation of $h_t[2]$ with $\max(0, x_0..x_{t-1})$ is 1.000 at $t = 2$, 0.994 at $t = 5$ and 0.958 at $t = 9$, with slope 17.5–17.8 at $t = 2$ and 5 and 16.4 at $t = 9$ ($1.73\times10.16 = 17.6$). The paper shows no such measurement and does not discuss the loss of fidelity by $t = 9$.
5. **Per-cell fits use an intercept $b$ in a bias-free network.** Every threshold is positively homogeneous of degree 1 in the earlier inputs, so $b$ can only stand for the average contribution of the other stored values over the $\mathcal N(0,1)$ sample. It is distribution-specific and not negligible: in the drift split $b$ changes by $-0.09$ and the runner-up term by $+0.14$, against $-0.28$ for the maximum's term and $-0.21$ in total. "Most of it comes from the sag of $n_4$'s gain" therefore hides two sizeable terms that partly cancel. The gains on the runner-up are never tabulated.
6. **The threshold fits are coarse on the scale that decides errors.** Residual standard deviations are 0.07–0.13, against a near-tie scale of 0.05. The paper's own Table 5 shows the cost: rules with "threshold errors of about 0.1" reproduce 67% of errors, while gate noise of 0.015 standard deviations reproduces 85%.
7. **"Follows from the weights" always assumes the modal gate pattern.** The patterns used cover 21% (resting; 51% with one-flip variants), 37% (age 1 after a new maximum) and 37% (new runner-up arrivals) of the relevant steps. Derived numbers are validated only by agreement with measured medians or means.
8. **The record formulas and the 0.7 rule are characterised only for a positive runner-up so far** (Figure 3b requires it to exceed 0.2). The paper says nothing about positive homogeneity or about negative values, apart from the step-1 thresholds and the costly route "first, $n_4$ silent" (runner-up negative in 97%, accuracy 29%, 8.5% of all errors).
9. **Every effect and coefficient is a derivative at the sequence's own gates.** $h_9 = \sum_p J_px_p$ is exact, but an input also acts by setting gates, which a trace does not show. "Most other inputs are not recorded and contribute almost nothing to the logits" is a statement about this attribution. The attributions also contain large cancelling terms (in the example a record of 0.3 gives $+20$ and $-15$ at the same logit).
10. **"Each comparator is essential"** comes from forcing a comparator off at every step, which leaves 10.6–18.4% of decisions, close to the 10% of uniform guessing. That shows necessity, not the specific role.
11. **What the timers do is not derived.** The $n_9$ intervention moves the maximum's median own effect from $-7.2$ to $-1.6$, but the path is not given, and item 1 shows that the stated path through $n_0$ is at best partial.

### 9.4 What the paper says, and does not say, about an off-centre circle and an ersatz bias

The paper never mentions an offset, a centre, or the need for units to stay positive. It treats every stored value as a trace through the origin whose "direction encodes its age". Its numbers nevertheless contain the ingredients, and my side-checks (in `check_decomposition_paper_weights.py`) add three facts.

1. **The maximum's trace has a large non-rotating part.** Splitting $w_4\,A^{a-1}D\,W_{hh}\,e_4$ along $A$'s eigenvectors, the norms of the components go from age 2 to age 8 as follows: 40.45 to 42.91 for the real mode 1.010, 10.09 to 8.59 for the real mode 0.973, and 27.97 to 19.53 for the $0.951\angle42.75°$ pair; every other component is at most 5.96. The eigenvectors are not orthogonal, so the norms do not add. The 1.010 eigen-component is positive on all 13 resting units. In the paper's linear picture the centre and the radius of the maximum's orbit are therefore both proportional to the same quantity, the new maximum's excess over $\theta_4$, and the centre is the part in the 1.010 and 0.973 modes. An inference I have not tested: since $\theta_4$ is near the old maximum, successive new maxima add increments, so the non-rotating parts add up to roughly the running maximum, while the newest rotating part is proportional to the newest excess, roughly the maximum minus the previous maximum. That would make the centre track the maximum and the newest radius track the gap between the top two.
2. **A record hardly excites the main clock** (paper, ✓W). My split of the record's own effect by age 2–6 gives 11.9, 12.6, 5.1, 19.0, 34.3 from the $0.94\angle83°$ pair; 33.8, 24.7, 27.0, 20.3, 12.9 from the $0.84\angle125°$ pair; $-3.9$, 21.9, 29.2, 24.0, 15.8 from the $0.74\angle43°$ pair; 4.3, 1.6, 4.5, 6.0, 10.2 from $-0.93$; and $-0.2$ to $-0.5$ from the $0.95\angle43°$ pair. So the age of the maximum and the age of a record are carried by different modes.
3. **Records are written as dips and read with negative weights.** $W_{hh}[:,5]$ is $\le 0$ on the tape units ($-0.91$ on $n_0$, $-0.66$ on $n_{15}$, $-0.52$ on $n_3$, $-0.42$ on $n_{14}$, $-0.14$ on $n_{12}$), while $W_{hh}[:,4]$ is positive on them ($1.76$ on $n_{10}$, $1.73$ on $n_2$, $1.71$ on $n_{11}$, $1.28$ on $n_{12}$, $0.87$ on $n_{14}$, $0.59$ on $n_0$). At its carrier a record's trace is negative and the readout weight is negative: $n_0$ at age 2, $-16.8\times(-2.90)$; $n_{12}$ at age 4, $-17.8\times(-2.79)$; $n_{11}$ at age 5, $-15.4\times(-4.56)$; $n_{10}$ and $n_{15}$ at age 6, $-16.7\times(-3.87)$ and $-24.9\times(-1.14)$. The exception is $n_3$ at age 3 ($+11.9\times1.70$). A dip can only be read while the unit stays on, so the tape needs a positive baseline, and in this picture the maximum's trace supplies it. That is the ersatz bias in your hypothesis, and §9.3 item 1 is a direct case of the baseline being exceeded.
4. **Observations in the paper that may be symptoms of this clipping** (untested): a new runner-up's arrival "pushes $n_0$, $n_3$ and $n_{15}$ down ... and switches some of them off"; "Older records fade faster than the resting map predicts"; and the unexplained switching of $n_3, n_{10}, n_{11}, n_{15}$, all four of which are record carriers.
