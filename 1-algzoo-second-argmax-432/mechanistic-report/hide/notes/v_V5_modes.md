# Verification bundle V5_modes

## Summary

Bundle V5 (M1-M6): no claim is refuted. M1-M4 reproduce to the printed digits from the weights alone. My M5/M6 cell tables (seeds 5010-5013, 4 x 200k sequences, own code) match the claims, and match the lead's saved table cell by cell (max difference 0.010 in |α|, 0.3° in phase, 0.005 in |β|, 0.002 in R²). All six are CONFIRMED; M5 carries one small caveat on two "about" moduli.

The interpretation holds in outline but needs five qualifications, each backed by a test that could have failed.

1. Level. level_t/w₄ ≈ 0.936·max(M_(t−1), 0), centred R² 0.993 over all states t ≥ 1; against max(M_t, 0) the centred R² is only 0.764. So it lags the maximum by one step and is clipped at 0. It is 0.99·M at a₁ = 1, drops to 0.92·M at a₁ = 2, then grows by 1.009-1.011 per step (eigenvalue 1.0099). It is the centre of the orbit for the impulse (cos 0.983 with the level eigenvector) but only roughly on data (cos 0.85-0.91; the level piece leaves 41-60% of the centre's norm, mostly runner and echo pieces).

2. Rotation. The phase of z is locked to a₁: φ = 0.5, 36.4, 79.3, 123.2, 166.0, 208.7, 251.0, 294.0, 337.8° for a₁ = 1..9, i.e. 42.97°/step from a₁ = 2 (max residual 0.6°), but the first step is 36°. The resting pattern holds in only 10.3% of states (never at a₁ = 1, 2; 2% at a₁ = 5, 6), and no sequence has more than 4 consecutive exact-linear steps. The law holds as well off the resting pattern as on it. Exact-linear steps rotate 0.952@42.8°, clipped steps 0.936@44.2° (43.6° / 45.5° / 49.6° with 1 / 2 / 3 units off), and the first step 1.009@33.4°.

3. Radius. Best single law is |z|/w₄ ≈ α(a₁)·(M − c·S⁺) with c = 0.87 falling to 0.75 over a₁ = 1..9. M − S is much better than M alone but not exact. For S < 0 the radius follows M, not M − S. At a tie about 0.15-0.2·M remains. After a runner-up event the radius follows the new gap only halfway (|ρ| ≈ 0.507·q + 0.487). The uncentred R² ≥ 0.945 is flattering: centred R² goes down to 0.79, and "M alone" already gets uncentred 0.81-0.85.

4. Readout. The comparator/wave cancellation (0.0575) is real, invariant under per-unit rescaling, and special (rank 1 of 715 four-unit splits; random vectors have median 0.75, none of 20,000 below 0.29). But "does not see" is too strong. Deleting the clock piece from h₉ changes 7.4% of answers (accuracy 95.3% → 90.6%); deleting only its comparator part changes 78.8%, only its wave part 55.0%. In logit[p₂] − logit[p₁] the clock's net slope is −6.3 logits per unit gap (= +74.2 comparator part − 80.5 wave part), growing from 0.0 at a₁ = 2 to −12.8 at a₁ = 9. The short mode (0.735, 42.99°) offsets it with +11.3. The total slope is +10.3.

5. Normalisation. M2's fractions are not invariant (h2 0.61 → 0.16-0.74, runner 0.51 → 0.05-0.50 across three natural normalisations). Only "clock, short, trans mostly on comparators; level on unit 2" survives. On data the comparators carry 78-83% of the clock coordinate, and the wave part is 23-69% of |z| in modulus. M4's sizes for complex pairs must be doubled to compare with real modes.

## Objections tested

- **Gauge and per-unit scale: 'fraction of a left vector on the comparators' (M2), the eigenvector entry ratios and cosine (M1) and 'size per unit coordinate' (M4) are not invariant under h_i → c_i·h_i, so they may say nothing about the mechanism.** (affects conclusion: partly)
  - test: (a) Random positive rescaling of all 16 units (seed 5002), recomputing every quantity. (b) Three natural normalisations: unit rms activation, unit std, unit-norm readout columns (seed 5030, 200k sequences). (c) A scale-invariant replacement for M2 measured on data: the share of each mode coordinate carried by a unit group, 1 − Σ|m − m_G|²/Σ|m|².
  - result: Invariant: eigenvalues, one-signedness of the level eigenvector, every U[:, i]·v_i product and the ratio 0.0575 in M3, and M5/M6 (coordinates divided by w₄). Not invariant: M2 (random rescaling: clock 0.966 → 0.932, level 0.041 → 0.103, h2 0.606 → 0.164, runner 0.506 → 0.321), and M4 except as size × w₄.
