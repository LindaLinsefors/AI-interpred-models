# Tape investigation, arm F_unit

## Answer

From my angle the answer is: the four-mode tape is not a self-contained store, but it is the part of the state that holds the comparison of every old input with the runner-up. Terms: a quiet state has $a_M \ge 2$, $a_S \ge 2$, $S_t > 0$; an old entry is a score entry of age $\ge 2$; the tape piece is $s^{\text{tape}} = U_o P_{\text{tape}} h[o]$ and the rest piece is the full score minus it (modes level, runner, clock, trans, echo, and the pulse units); "ranks the runner-up first" means the runner-up's entry is the largest old entry. (1) The value comparison is in the tape alone: near the runner-up, (entry $-$ runner-up's entry) has slope 71.6 logits per unit of $x - S$ in the tape piece, 74.2 in the full score and 2.6 in the rest piece; the short clock carries 57% of that slope, h3 23%, h2 16%, alt 4%. (2) The tape piece also carries an offset that does not depend on the entry's own input and that the rest cancels: alone it ranks the runner-up first in 83.9% of quiet final states (full score 97.3%), with a comparison error of $0.121\,M$ (sd) against $0.039\,M$. (3) No single part completes it (trans +3.8 points, runner +3.5, clock +2.3, level, echo and pulses 0 or below); runner + clock + trans + echo are needed for 96.8%, level adds the last 0.5, the pulse units nothing, and which part helps most changes with the configuration. (4) What the rest adds is an offset pattern over the old entries that depends on the two ages $(a_M, a_S)$ jointly, is linear in $M$ and $S$, and has a term linear in the two newest inputs $x_t, x_{t-1}$; substituting exactly that description for the true rest piece gives 95.9% (deleting it 84.0%), while a description by order of arrival alone or by one age alone gives 88–91%. (5) The reason is visible in the weights: a write (a pulse of unit 5 or 4) spreads over all nine modes, and the tape's share of it is the bump at the written entry plus a false lobe at the oldest entries, which the transient mode cancels for two steps; for a new maximum there is also a slow lobe on the entries older than the maximum, cancelled by level + runner + clock. On data, with a runner-up two steps old the tape piece fails in 49.6% of states and puts the age-9 entry on top in 78% of those. (6) Functionally the tape is what the network needs to name the runner-up among old inputs and not what it needs to locate the maximum: deleting it from a quiet state at step 5–8 changes 94–97% of answers when the top two stay the top two, 30–31% when the maximum is later overtaken (deleting the clock: 7–9% and 88–90%), and 4–6% when the answer arrives later. (7) Each tape mode's piece is 70–78% determined by $(M, S, a_M, a_S)$ and 86–92% by the top three values and their ages; the single modes are not separately meaningful, because replacing one by that description costs 0.5–2.5 points while replacing all four together costs nothing. The short clock's part that is locked to the main clock exists with the reported size (0.12–0.27 $w_4$ per unit gap) but is at most 9% of that coordinate's variance; half of the variance follows the age of the runner-up. (8) No redefinition by modes helps: the four modes are the best of all 126 four-mode sets, only sets of 8 or 9 modes reach 96%, the subspace of the shift matrix $\Sigma$ is worse (70.3%), and the best linear decoder I fitted on the 7 tape coordinates reaches 90.4% (97.7% from all 13 coordinates). The object that is self-contained is the readout's own view, $f_t(a) = s_t[9-a] - s_t[9-a_S]$ over old entries, which also survives a quiet step with half the change of the tape piece; it does not make single entries cleaner functions of named quantities (71% of entry variance against 69%). Not established: why the offsets depend on both ages jointly, what the last 10% of each tape mode is, and any sufficiency claim beyond the final readout (my substitutions were at the final step only). I did not write tape_v2.md; these conclusions are for the synthesis step.

## Claims (10)

### 1. [strong] The four-mode tape piece is not self-contained: alone it ranks the runner-up first among old entries in 83.9% of quiet final states against 97.3% for the full score, and no single other part closes the gap. Four of the five other modes are needed; the pulse units contribute nothing.

- evidence: 369,571 quiet final states (t = 9) from 600,000 N(0,1) sequences, seed 101. P(runner-up's entry is the largest of ages >= 2): tape alone 0.839 (on top instead: maximum 3.5%, third 6.3%, another input 6.3%); + trans 0.877, + runner 0.874, + clock 0.862, + pulse 0.839, + level 0.836, + echo 0.831. Best pair runner+trans 0.907; best triple runner+clock+trans 0.948; best four runner+clock+trans+echo 0.968; all five modes 0.973; adding pulses 0.973. Agreement of the top entry with the full score's: 0.845, 0.960, 0.985, 0.999 for tape alone, best triple, best four, five modes. Pooled over steps t = 3..9 (429,664 states, 150,000 sequences, seed 102): tape 0.901, full 0.978, same best triple (0.968) and best four (0.975); best single there is clock 0.923 = runner 0.923, trans 0.919.

### 2. [strong] The value comparison with the runner-up is held by the tape alone; the rest adds offsets only. The tape's missing precision is an offset error, not a missing slope.

- evidence: Same population. Entries of age >= 2 whose input is within 0.1 M below S; (entry - runner-up's entry)/M fitted linearly in (x - S)/M. Slope: tape piece 71.6, full score 74.2, rest piece alone 2.6. Residual spread divided by the slope (error in the input): sd 0.121 M (tape) -> 0.090 (+trans) -> 0.071 (+runner+trans) -> 0.049 (+runner+clock+trans) -> 0.039 (full); half interquartile range 0.051 M (tape) -> 0.032 (+runner+clock) -> 0.0285 (+level+runner+clock) -> 0.021 (full). So trans removes outliers and the slow modes tighten the typical error. Slope of single modes alone: short 40.5, h3 16.4, h2 11.8, alt 3.0.
- script: hide/t1_selfcontained/s1_subsets.py -> s1_subsets.txt

## Did not work

- Prediction 'without fresh writes the tape plus the slow modes is complete' failed: with a_M >= 4, a_S >= 4 and x_7, x_8, x_9 < 0.5 S (157,272 states) the tape alone gives 0.918, + level+runner+clock 0.950, full 0.978; adding trans alone lowers it to 0.877 and trans + echo gives 0.911, so trans and echo are needed together even when unit 5 has not fired recently (s7 part B).
- The subspace of the shift matrix Sigma as a tape definition: worse than the four modes on every measure (0.703 against 0.839 for ranking, 0.855 against 0.951 for persistence).
- Searching the 511 subsets of modes for a smaller self-contained store: none below 8 modes reaches 0.96.
- Describing the rest piece by something coarser than both ages jointly (no cells, order of arrival, a_M alone, a_S alone): 0.881-0.913 against 0.958.
- Reading the short clock as mainly a copy of the main clock: at most 9% of its variance.
- Free evolution lambda^(a_S) as the encoding of the runner-up's age: roughly right in phase for h3 and for the first three steps of h2, wrong for the short clock, and absent when the maximum is two steps old.
- 'True rest + tape fitted on (M, S) per (a_M, a_S)' as a sufficiency test of the tape's contents is partly circular (the cells contain a_S) and returns 0.977, above the network's own 0.973; it cannot show that the tape stores nothing else.
- The logistic decoders did not converge within 400 iterations, so the decoder accuracies are lower bounds.
- s2_completion.py runs for 3 min 52 s, over the 3-minute target (per-entry fits in 336 cells for 13 pieces).

## Open

- Why the rest's offset depends on (a_M, a_S) jointly: a split into a term from the maximum's write (slow lobe on entries older than the maximum, in level+runner+clock) and a term from the runner-up's write was seen in the weights-level kernel but not fitted on data.
- The 1.0-1.4 points of ranking that remain after the best description of the rest piece (0.959-0.963 against 0.973), and the roughly 10% of each tape mode's piece that the top three values and ages do not explain.
- Why deleting the tape changes 30% of answers when the old maximum is the final answer.
- Whether the top-three description of the tape survives when the run continues (my substitutions were at the final readout only), and with donor states instead of cell-wise fits; the transplant and resampling investigators cover this.
- Why trans and echo are both needed on old entries when the two newest inputs are far below the runner-up: I assume the newest inputs' immediate response leaks into the tape modes through closed comparator gates, but did not test it.
- Whether a nonlinear decoder recovers the runner-up's position from the 7 tape coordinates alone (linear: at least 0.904).
- The functional weight of the short clock's clock-locked part in the runner-up's margin over the maximum (report section 4.3: +11.3 logits per unit gap) was not re-measured.
