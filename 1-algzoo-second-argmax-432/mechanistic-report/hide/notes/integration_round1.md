# Integration notes: round-1 arms (lead's digest of notes/r1_*.md)

Full structured results per arm are in `notes/r1_<arm>.md` (dumped from the workflow journal by `dump_arm_results.py`); raw tables are in `hide/r1_<arm>/*_out*.txt`.

## Arm F (record event) — key results
- Record at step $s$ (old max $M_0$ of age $g$, old runner-up $S_0$): unit 4 on (99.997%), all four comparators off (95.5%); units 5 and 9 fire at $s+1$ (100%, 99.7%); unit 0 is off at $s+2$ (99.7% when the following inputs are low) and only there; resting pattern at $s+3$.
- $h_4(s) = 10.157\,(x_s - \theta_4)$ exactly; $h_5(s+1) \approx 12.2\,(M_n - M_0) + 10.4\,M_0 - 5.8\,S_0^+$; $h_9(s+1) \approx 1.56\,(M_n - M_0) + 1.77\,M_0 - 0.43\,S_0^+$. Exact: $W[5,4]w_4 = 12.057$, $W[9,4]w_4 = 1.481$.
- One step after the record: $\theta_7 \approx 1.018 M_n$, $\theta_4 \approx 0.985 M_n$, $\theta_1 \approx 0.83 M_n + 0.16 M_0$, $\theta_6 \approx 0.98 M_0$, $\theta_8 \approx 1.07 M_0$.
- **Unit 5 is an anti-kick**: in the readout-visible modes its write is 168°–189° opposite to unit 4's one-step-rotated kick and of similar size, and nearly absent in level and clock. With the clipping of unit 0 at $s+2$, two steps after a record the new gap is held only in level ($0.92\,w_4$), runner ($2.2\,w_4$) and clock ($1.04\,w_4$ at 36°); 4%, 11%, 8% of the kick remain in h2, alt, h3 (37% in short).
- Unit 0 is clipped at $s+2$ by the pulses: $-0.913\,h_5 = -12.9$, $-1.424\,h_9 = -3.3$ against $+15.5$ from wave units; without both pulse terms it would be on in 100%.
- Comparator clipping at the record erases the old clock (5–31% left) and rewrites the runner mode from $S_0$ to $M_0$.
- **Deposit**: from the weights, the comparator-zeroed clock state peaks exactly at the old max's position in 35/35 cells (ages 2..9, 0..6 steps later), 4.0–10.6 logits per unit radius. On data (relative to bystander positions): old max existed 13.5, created +28.3 (clock part of the comparator clipping +28.0) → 41.8; old runner-up 20.1 → 15.9; new max 20.9. Created ≈ $47.6\,(M_0 - S_0) + 11.8\,S_0 + 2.9\,(M_n - M_0)$.
- **The old max's own unit-5 pulse (one step after it arrived) is necessary**: removing it drops P(answer = old max) from 0.967 to 0.020 although the record still creates the same +28 bump (its pre-record score goes from +12.3 above bystanders to −12.6 below). The new record's unit-5 pulse is not needed for the old max (0.973 → 0.927).
- Interventions after every record: unit 5 off at $s+1$: accuracy 0.493; unit 9 off: 0.665 (87.5% of changed answers go to the max); unit 0 forced on at $s+2$: 0.763 (66% to the max). Units 5 and 9 both off after the final record: never answers the max, old max still found in 94.3%.
- Zeroing the clock one step before the record: P(old max) 0.97 → 0.103. Rotating the clock by $k$: answer moves to $p_0 - k$ in 28–40% (never the wrong way); rotating all five rotating modes: 55–74%. Clock-only rotation works in 76.5% for the top tercile of the old gap, 11.5% for the bottom.
- Nothing erases the old runner-up (its score falls 4.2 on average, sd 22; a random bystander is perturbed as much). It loses because the old max gains ≈ 28.
- Record at $s = 9$: no pulses; the clipping acts at the record step itself (reading the comparators unclipped flips the choice among old positions from the old max 0.952 to the old runner-up 0.914); unit 4 read directly ($U[9,4] = -4.99$). Record at $s = 8$: pulses read directly ($U[8,5] = +3.40$, $U[8,9] = -1.66$).
- Corrections to my earlier statements: (i) free-flow pulse kernels do not describe the running network after a record (units 5, 9 and unit 0's clipping cancel the kick's visible content within two steps); the standing max sits 13.5 logits above bystanders and 6.6 below the runner-up; (ii) $\rho(9) \approx 0.80$ (not 0.72); (iii) level $R^2$ is 0.987–0.993 at ages 6–9.

## Arm H (runner-up events) — key results
- $\theta_j = S + \rho_j(a)(M - S)$; medians of $\rho_j$ for $a = 1..9$: unit 1: 0.84, −0.04, 0.26, 0.82, 1.29, 1.40, 1.12, 0.61, 0.15; unit 6: −0.05, 0.67, 1.05, 1.19, 1.07, 0.81, 0.52, 0.37, 0.42; unit 7: 1.02, 1.02, 1.01, 0.87, 0.69, 0.58, 0.58, 0.69, 0.85; unit 8: −0.02, 0.32, 0.06, 0.05, 0.42, 0.97, 1.39, 1.45, 1.14; unit 4: 0.98, 0.97, 0.95, 0.86, 0.74, 0.65, 0.64, 0.72, 0.83.
- Which comparators a new runner-up clips is set by the age of the max: ages 1–4 at least one threshold is at ≈ $S$ and is clipped; at ages 5–8 most new runners-up clip **no** comparator (65, 91, 83, 55%).
- **Unit 5 is a fifth comparator** (exact): $z_5(t+1) = w_5 x_{t+1} + 18.009\,(x_t - \Theta_5) - 10.462\,[x_t - \theta_8]_+ - 7.813\,[x_t - \theta_7]_+ + 0.167\,[x_t - \theta_6]_+ - 0.005\,[x_t - \theta_1]_+ +$ wave clips. Slope in $x_t$: 18.0 (neither 7 nor 8 clipped), 7.5 (8 clipped), 10.2 (7 clipped), −0.27 (both: saturated).
- $\Theta_5 = 0.01265\,m_{runner} - 0.02872\,m_{level} + $ small $\approx (0.70..0.86)\,S - (0.01..0.07)\,M$; blind to the clock (≤ 0.037 gap). Rule "unit 5 on iff $x_t > 0.75 S$" is right for 97.4% of below-runner-up inputs. P(on) = 0.1% for negative inputs, 51% for $x/S \in [0.7, 0.8)$, 89% for $[0.8, 0.9)$, 99.5% for $[0.9, 1)$.
- Unit 5 fires after 99.9% of new runners-up; unit 9 after 0.34% of them (99.2% after new maxima, 50.5% after misfires of unit 4). Unit 9 is vetoed by comparator 7 ($W[9,7] g_7 = 66.3$ per unit).
- A new runner-up writes $+5.7..6.5\,K(x_t - S)$ into the runner mode at max ages 1–4 ($2.6..3.8$ later); shrinks the clock by $(0.84, 0.83, 0.75, 0.53)\,K(x_t - S)$ at ages 1–4 and essentially not at ages ≥ 5. $\Theta_5$ follows a new runner-up with gain 0.58–0.73 at ages 1–4 and 0.36–0.41 at ages 5–6.
- **Clock radius law refined**: Re$(b_S/b_M)$ = −0.90..−0.94 when the runner-up arrived 1 step after the max, −0.88, −0.80..−0.85, −0.70..−0.74, −0.47..−0.52, −0.38, −0.28 for 2..7 steps after. (When the runner-up is the old max the launch gives $M - S$ directly.)
- Logit bump of a new runner-up at its own position: +42..48 per unit $x_t$ (max ages 1–4), +57..61 (ages 5–8); 74–100% from the unit-5 pulse; old runner-up's position falls by 9–23 per unit. Final margin over the old runner-up ≈ $58..72\,(x_t - S)$, with the $S$- and $M$-terms cancelling.
- Records just below the runner-up are harmful on net: forcing unit 5 off after every below-runner-up input raises accuracy 95.30 → 95.89% (uniform 93.83 → 94.59%). Nearly all the gain is from $0.9 \le x/S < 1$.
- Third value: margin between runner-up's and third's positions ≈ $c\,(S - T)$, $c$ = 77 (order T, M, S), 50 (M, T, S), 64–66 (M, S, T); on N(0,1): 77.6, 54.0, 63.9, 72.7 (T,S,M), 51.8 (S,T,M), 67.3 (S,M,T); offsets ≤ 2.4. "Answered the third" is 2.98% of sequences = 64% of errors.
- **No second clock**: each written mode then rotates/decays freely with its own eigenvalue (median $R^2 \ge 0.998$).
- Unit 4 misfires in 9.9% of inputs that land between $S$ and $M$ (near-certain for the top 20% of the gap at max ages 5–8); removing misfires: accuracy 95.33 → 95.67% (uniform 93.80 → 94.44%).
- Position of a runner-up that arrived after the max is carried by h3 (largest at ages 2–4), short (ages ≥ 3), h2; the runner mode ($\approx 6KS$) is position-blind except −0.78 at position 9.
- Low inputs are not perfectly traceless: every all-on input adds $1.026\,x_t$ to the runner coordinate (shifts $\Theta_5$ by $0.013\,x_t$) and $-0.0447\,x_t$ to the level.

## Arm B (max circuit) — key results
- $\theta_4 = (0.7956\,\text{level} + 0.0353\,\text{runner} + \text{clock term} + \dots)/10.157 + 0.0970\,h_4 + \dots$; slow part $0.82 M + 0.16 S^+$; clock term amplitude $0.16\,\rho(a) \times$ gap, positive at ages 1–2, negative at ages 5–6. Impulse: next threshold 0.988, 0.979, 0.963, 0.881, 0.780, 0.717, 0.726, 0.795, 0.885 at ages 0..8.
- On data $M - \theta_4 = \varepsilon(a) M + \kappa(a)(M - S^+)$, $\kappa$ = 0.003, 0.002, 0.023, 0.100, 0.196, 0.256, 0.253, 0.201, 0.124 (ages 0..8), $\varepsilon$ = 0.010–0.062.
- Unit 4 on for 99.936% of positive records, 1.112% of other inputs (new runners-up in the upper part of the gap: 19–29% of them at ages 4–6); never on for a record with $x_t \le 0$.
- Level (exact): $\text{level}(t) = 1.00989\,\text{level}(t-1) + h_4(t-1) - 0.0724\,h_5(t-1) - 0.1263\,h_9(t-1) - 0.0447\,x_t + $ clips. From rest: 1.006 one step after the record, 0.927 two steps after (unit 5 takes 0.087, unit 9 0.019), then +1%/step for an isolated max; flat 0.92–0.94 on data because unit 5 fires after each new runner-up.
- Negative values: accuracy 0.119 / 0.231 / 0.979 with 0 / 1 / 2 positive inputs; ≤ 1 positive input is 1.07% of sequences and 18% of all errors. Soft floor: accuracy 0.60 just below $S = 0$, 0.68 just above, 0.95 at $S/M \in (0.05, 0.1)$. Thresholds of a max with a negative runner-up are those of an isolated max.
- Unit 2: $h_2/(10.157 M)$ = 1.74, 1.73, 1.82, 1.79 at ages 1–4, then 1.67, 1.53, 1.45, 1.46, 1.56 (follows the clock). Not a standalone integrator (cutting its other inputs: accuracy 83.0%).
- Level scaling by $f$ at step $t_0$: thresholds shift by 0.63–0.80 of the change; ±10% changes 12–17% of answers, ±20% 20–31%; for $f > 1$ the new answer is the true max in 75–85% of changes.
- Replacing unit 4 by the oracle $10.157\,[x_t - \max(M,0)]_+$: agreement 96.3%, accuracy **93.1%** (lower). Its gate helps (+0.34 pp), its size hurts (the record update is a closed loop on the network's own sagged threshold, $\theta_4 \leftarrow \theta_4 + 0.986\,(x - \theta_4)$).


## Arm I (errors) — key results
- Accuracy $0.95290 \pm 0.00015$ on $2^{21}$ sequences. Of the errors: 63.6% answer the third, 20.2% the max, 16.2% fourth or lower.
- 92.4% of errors fall in two classes: runner-up not positive (17.8% of errors; accuracy 0.233 with one positive input, 0.123 with none) or a relative gap below $0.05 M$ (74.6%: 54.1% runner-up ≈ third, 15.7% max ≈ runner-up, 4.9% both). With $T > 0$ and both gaps $\ge 0.2 M$ (22.4% of sequences) accuracy is 99.97%.
- Shift: accuracy under $x + c$ = 0.116, 0.134, 0.547, 0.859, 0.953, 0.952, 0.940, 0.912, 0.881 for $c$ = −3, −2, −1, −0.5, 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3. Unif(−1,1): 0.9384. The reference is zero (all five thresholds are exactly 0 at $t = 0$); a runner-up at $S = sM$ is found with probability 0.15, 0.31, 0.51, 0.83, 0.93, 0.96 at $s$ = −0.25, −0.10, −0.05, 0, 0.05, 0.10. Re-weighting one error table over the two relative gaps predicts accuracy on shifted Gaussian and uniform inputs within about 1 pp.
- Interventions ($2^{21}$): unit 4 := oracle 0.9314 (agreement 0.9633; fixes 14.3% of errors, adds 60.1%); unit-4 misfires removed 0.9563; unit 5 off after inputs below the runner-up 0.9588; both 0.9621 (fixes 23.8%, adds 4.2%). Misfire removal repairs 64% of the class "max ≈ runner-up, max first"; unit-5 removal 41% of "runner-up ≈ third, runner-up first"; the mirror classes and the non-positive runner-up are not repaired.
- Near ties runner-up/third: coefficients of $S$ and $T$ in the margin are nearly equal and opposite (+70..+80, −69..−80); linear over $\pm 0.15 M$; but the zero is displaced: a later candidate takes over from an earlier one when it exceeds it by $\varepsilon^* M$, quartiles −0.010 / −0.001 / +0.011, sd 0.0235. **70% of var($\varepsilon^*$) is a fixed function of the three positions** $(p_e, p_l, p_M)$ (cross-validated), reproduced by three-value inputs on a constant background ($r = 0.91$, 360 configurations). Slope 24–46 when the two candidates are adjacent and old, 100–155 when about four positions apart and old. Offsets of 5–11% of $M$ in specific configurations (third at 7 vs runner-up at 0 with the max last; runner-up at 8 vs third at 0 with the max at 9; runner-up right after the third at positions 3, 4).
- Max vs later input: twice as sharp; the level at which a later input is treated as the new max stays within about 2% of $M$ although $\theta_4$ sags to 0.70–0.84 $M$ (the readout compensates unit 4's small misfires).
- Ages: accuracy is not lower for an old runner-up ($a_2 = 5..8$: 0.958–0.965); lowest when the max is last (0.935) or first (0.931).
- The resting gate pattern (all 13 on, pulses off) holds in only 9.3% of states at $t = 1..8$; units 0, 1, 3, 6, 7, 8, 15 are each off in 18–27% of states.
- Eight hand-checkable failing inputs (I6) with mechanisms: zero reference; false record through sagged $\theta_4$; missed record; later third wins through unit 5; late runner-up fails to displace an earlier third; etc. (see notes/r1_I_errors.md).


## Arm E (exact source decomposition of the logits) — key results
- Exact identity ($10^{-13}$): $h_9[O] = \sum_s A^{9-s}[w_O x_s + W_{OP}\,p_{s-1} + c_s]$, $c_s = $ clip amounts $\ge 0$; comparator clip $c_s[j] = g_j[x_s - \theta_j(s)]_+$. Logits = sum of fixed kernels × amplitudes.
- Kernels (exact): raw input: +57.19 on position 9 at age 0, ≤ 1.01 afterwards (97% gone in one step). Pulse 4: negative tape (−4.99, −6.63 at ages 0, 1; −3.2..−4.1 later) at its own position, no decay. Pulse 5: positive tape +3.37..+4.16 at the position of the input one step before, +0.85..1.37 at the next position, −0.4..−2.4 on the 2–4 positions before. Pulse 9: trough −2.8..−3.4 one position before the causing input (ages 2–7). Single comparator clips: travelling waves of size 40–120 per unit excess; their sum over the four comparators cancels the raw input (≤ 2.1 after one step). Wave-unit clips: troughs at fixed offsets (unit 0 at own−2, 12 at own−4, 11 at own−5, 10 at own−6).
- Composites: CLIP = raw input + comparator clips; MARK = pulse 4 + wave-unit clips; TAPE = pulse 5. Mean contributions to the margin runner-up − max (CLIP / MARK / TAPE / P9 = total): runner-up arrived before the max (RM): 25.7 / 5.0 / −14.3 / 1.8 = 18.2; after (MR): −14.8 / 67.3 / −32.2 / −3.0 = 17.3. Runner-up − third: RM 29.3 / −27.4 / 18.5 / 1.6 = 22.0; MR 3.1 / 2.0 / 16.1 / −0.6 = 20.6.
- So in this linear-propagation bookkeeping: when the runner-up came after the max, the margin over the max is carried by the record mark (unit 4 plus the wave-unit clips at max ages 2, 4, 5, 6, which sit on the max's position: −13 at age 2 to −27..−30 from age 6); when the runner-up is an overtaken max, it is carried by the comparator clips at the new max's arrival (+26.5 on the old max's position; lands on the old max in 88.9%; size uncorrelated with the new max's excess, $R^2$ 0.002; slope 42 on the old gap, $R^2$ 0.53).
- At a record's own position pulses 4 and 5 nearly cancel with pulse 5 larger (+15.6 RM, +21.0 MR).
- Wave unit 0 is clipped with probability 0.998 when the running max is exactly two steps old (≤ 0.007 at ages 1, 3, 4, 5).
- **Last step (exact):** $L_9 = -49.90[\theta_7 - x_9]_+ - 7.38[\theta_6 - x_9]_+ - 5.01[\theta_1 - x_9]_+ + 4.04[\theta_8 - x_9]_+ - 50.70[x_9 - \theta_4]_+ + \rho_9$. $\partial(L_9 - L_p)/\partial x_9$ = 61.0–62.1 for all $p \le 8$ below the thresholds, −54.6..−57.8 above all of them. For a fixed prefix the network answers 9 on a single interval of $x_9$ whose edges are the stored runner-up (slope 1.013, $R^2$ 0.990) and the stored max (slope 0.998, $R^2$ 0.996). Position 8 is scored through unit 5 in the same currency (62.1 per unit).
- Readout-weight interventions: $U[8,5] := 0$: accuracy with the runner-up at position 8 falls 0.944 → 0.017; $U[9,7] := 0$: overall 0.548; $U[9,4] := 0$: max-last 0.935 → 0.775.
- Not sparse: with exact kernels, dropping any class costs a lot (all but P9: agreement 0.886; all but wave clips 0.456; all but P4 0.353; all but P5 0.118). Refitted kernels: X + P4 + P5 + comparator clips: agreement 0.909; plus wave clips: 0.984.
- Level + clock on data: 19% of the rms (3.7% of the sum of squares) of the logit differences among positions 0–8; removing them changes the best position among 0–8 in 9.4% of sequences. Position 8's level readout is −0.168 (outside the −0.31..−0.53 I quoted).


## Arm D (geometry; the user's hypothesis) — key results
- **Directions fixed: supported.** Clock phase $\varphi(a)$ = 36, 79, 123, 166, 209, 251, 294, 337° for $a = 2..9$, independent of $M$ and of $a_2$ (within ±8° in all 72 $(a_1, a_2)$ cells). Nearest-phase decoding of the age: 98.9–99.9% for $S/M \le 0.4$, 96% for 0.4–0.6, 84% for 0.6–0.8, 38% for $S/M > 0.9$.
- **Centre depends on $M$ and $S$: supported, two terms.** centre $\approx 10.157\,[\ell M R_{level} + (r_0 M + r_1 S) R_{runner}]$, $\ell$ = 0.90–1.01, $r_0$ = 2.9 → 1.55 ($a$ = 2 → 9), $r_1 \approx 5.2$. The runner eigenvector has mixed sign: it lowers the centre of units 0, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15 by 6–10 per unit of $S$ and raises unit 2 and the comparators.
- **Radius: depends on $M$ and $S$, but not exactly $\propto M - S$.** $|z|/10.157 \approx \rho(a)(M - S) + \delta S$, $\rho(a) \approx 1.02 \times 0.951^{a-2}$ (free decay within 2% at small $S/M$), $\delta$ = 0.07–0.39 by cell. Exact two-pulse runs: (1,0,0,0,2,…) gives $|z| = 1.22\,\rho(a)(M-S)$; (2,0,0,0,1,…) gives $1.51\,\rho(a)(M-S)$. In 13-dim Euclidean norm the mean orbit has radius ≈ $23.5\,(M - 0.63 S)$.
- $u(a)$ (state per unit of $M - S$) for $a = 2..9$: 99.73% of its variance in a plane 5.1°/7.9° from the clock plane; rotation 42.1° per step; centre has cosine 0.992 with the level eigenvector. $v(a)$ (state per unit $S$ at $M = S$) also rotates (96% planar, 17° from the clock plane, about half the radius).
- Regression $h[\mathrm{ORD}] \sim (M-S)u(a_1) + S v(a_1)$: centred $R^2$ 0.44–0.54; with $x_t$: 0.82–0.92; $(M-S, S, T, x_t)$ per $(a_1, a_2)$ cell: 0.94–0.98.
- **(a) off-centre because of the ReLUs: supported with two qualifications.** Without the centre 48–55% of the (unit, age) entries of the orbit are negative, with it 4–6%. But the orbit is tangent to the ReLU boundary: at $S = 0$ the clock radius is within ±22% of the centre for 9 of 13 units (0, 1, 3, 8, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15; ≤ 0.45 for units 2, 6, 7, 13); units 10 and 11 dip just below zero at max ages 6 and 5 for every $S/M$ (pre-activation ≈ −0.5 $M$; OFF 67–98%). These grazing cuts change the clock by ≤ 2% (its left vector is ≤ 0.22 on wave units).
- **(b) exact; (c) supported.** A linear map that rotates about $c \ne 0$ must fix $c$; $A$ has two real eigenvalues near 1 (1.0099, 0.9735); the level eigenvector is positive although $A$ has 67 negative entries of 169; the level coordinate is 0.92–0.94 × 10.157 $M$ and supplies 80% of unit 4's threshold.
- **Interventions on the centre** (level × $f$ at step $t_0$ = 3, 5, 7, max age ≥ 2; later inputs made low): accuracy 97% → 11 / 21 / 20% ($f = 0$), 30–37% ($f = 0.5$), 66–68% ($f = 0.75$), 87–94% ($f = 1.25$), 85–91% ($f = 1.5$). With $f = 0$: 5.5–6.0 units newly off at the next step; clock amplitude ratio after one step 0.54 / 0.76 / 0.93, phase +26..+37°, then it keeps rotating; the ReLU regenerates 25–30% of the level.
- **Controls:** clock × 0 costs 5–6 points and clips nothing when the top two do not change later; with real inputs erasing the clock costs 3 points if the max is kept and about 44 points if the max is later replaced. Clock × 1.5 clips 1.6 extra units and costs 15–22 points.
- Impulse plane (the user's earlier analysis) reproduced: $|n|$ = 34.99, in-plane radius 22.10, rotation 41.47°/step; the centre is the level mode (runner −8%), the rotation is the clock mode (short −6%).
- Second rotating component (h2, h3, alt, short): coordinate of the $S$ coefficient $= F_m(a_1 - a_2)\,\lambda_m^{\min(a_1,a_2)-2}$: its phase tracks the more recent of the two top-two arrivals; amplitude closest to $S - T$.
- The exactly resting gate pattern holds in 18.8% of states with max age ≥ 2 (1.2% at age 2, 3–4% at ages 5–6, 54–64% at ages 7–9); the rms ReLU correction to the resting map is 6.6% of the state norm for max age ≥ 3.


## Arm C (clock) — key results
- **Phase law confirmed.** $\varphi(a_1)$ = 0.3, 36.5, 79.5, 123.3, 166.0, 208.6, 251.0, 294.0, 337.7° for $a_1 = 1..9$; the same in every $(a_1, a_2)$ cell to 3.3° ($a_1 \le 5$) or 6.8° ($a_1 = 6..9$). The phase of $z$ lags $\varphi(a_1)$ more as the gap closes. Nearest-phase decoding of the age from $\arg z$: 84.0% overall (97.4% within one step); by $(M - S^+)/M$: 27.7%, 46.0%, 67.9%, 89.1%, 99.0%, 99.0% for 0–0.05, 0.05–0.10, 0.10–0.25, 0.25–0.50, 0.50–0.75, 0.75–1.
- **Radius law is a first approximation.** $(M - S^+)$ explains 94.9% of $\sum|\hat z|^2$, $M$ alone 82.7%, free $\alpha M + \beta S^+$ 97.1% with $\beta/\alpha$ = −0.75..−0.88; per-cell three-term fit ($M - S^+$, $S^+$, $T^+$) 99.2%. Exact traces: the runner-up's coefficient is −(0.63..1.0) times the max's when the runner-up is the previous max, −(0.77..0.93) when it arrived 1–3 steps after the max, −0.64..−0.69 at 4 steps, about 0 at 5–7 steps.
- **A record does not erase the clock completely.** Exact: zeroing the four comparators in a pure clock state keeps $0.494\,e^{-i68°}$ of the clock coordinate (the clock's right vector is mostly on wave units), and leaks 0.138 of the level, 0.465 of short, 0.681 of trans into it. By intervention: removing the pre-record clock changes the post-record clock by $0.341\,e^{i28°} z(s-1)$. All four comparators are zero at 94.2% of records (74–83% when the old max is 4–7 steps old). The kick is $h_4/K = M_{new} - \theta_4$, and $\theta_4$ is below the old max by up to 43% of the old gap, so the kick overshoots the true increment.
- **First step is 37°, exact:** $z(s+2)/z(s+1) = \lambda + c_5 W[5,4] + c_9 W[9,4] = 0.951@42.7° + 0.088@{-137.9°} + 0.133@{-4.4°} = 0.958@37.0°$.
- **New runner-up $v$:** comparator $j$ is cut iff $v > \theta_j$ and then adds $c_j (v - \theta_j)$ to $\hat z$, $c_j$ = 0.800@−123.9° (unit 1), 1.308@159.2° (6), 1.377@28.7° (7), 0.670@−44.6° (8). Immediate radial update as a fraction of the ideal, by max age 1..9: 0.83, 0.72, 0.75, 0.63, 0.14, 0.04, 0.00, 0.20, 0.46.
