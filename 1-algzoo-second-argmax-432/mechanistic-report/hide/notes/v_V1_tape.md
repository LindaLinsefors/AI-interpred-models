# Verification bundle V1_tape

## Summary

All five claims T1–T5 are CONFIRMED: every number reproduces from my own code (T1 and T5 exactly, T2 and T3 within 0.001, T4 within 1 %). No bug found in the lead's e10, e12, e13 (read only after my numbers existed). The interpretation survives only in a narrower form.

What survives the attacks
- Largest valid entry of U h_t = runner-up so far, given S_t > 0: 0.961–0.992, flat in the age of the runner-up (0.949–0.971 at t = 9), so not driven by the newest positions. Chance and 'guess the newest' are both 1/(t+1).
- Σ is not a shift by accident. In five null models (random A, Q A Qᵀ, random U, column-permuted U, rotated row space; 300 draws each) no draw comes near it: mean cos(u_i A, u_(i+1)) over i = 0..6 is 0.987 for the network, 0.00 ± 0.10–0.20 for the nulls (largest 0.65).
- The slope is a property of the weights, not of the data: (U[9] − U[i])·w[ORD] = 61.0–62.1 for every i = 0..8; per-state derivative 61.9 ± 0.4; 61.5–62.0 under six other input distributions. The tie point sits at x_t = S − 0.013·M (median), sd 0.03·M.

What does not hold as worded
1. 'A step without an event shifts the tape' holds only for entries at least two steps old. On real no-event steps the centred R² of s_t against s_(t−1) shifted by one is negative over all old positions (the newest entry is rescaled by about 0.15–0.24, correlation 0.40), and 0.50, 0.56, 0.74, 0.89, 0.93, 0.94, 0.96 (t = 3..9) for entries of age ≥ 2. The max's own entry also moves in its first two steps (−21.7 → −14.2 → −4.2 in units of M).
2. T1/T2 describe the resting map, which is the acting map in only 5–29 % of real states (13 ordinary units on, pulses off). U_o A h[ORD] predicts the real next scores worse than the bare shift (R² &lt; 0 for t ≤ 5; 0.56–0.86 for t = 6..9); it needs the pulse-unit term W[ORD,PULSE] h[PULSE] to match the shift. The ordinary-unit tape alone gives T3 accuracy 0.50–0.83 instead of 0.96–0.99.
3. 'Comparisons by value with slope 61' is true for the newest input only. For entries of age ≥ 2 the own value explains 16.5 % of the variance of the score; the configuration (t, age, age of max, age of runner-up) explains 46.6 %. The score is a clipped comparison: slope about 60–80 per unit within roughly 0.15–0.2·M below the runner-up, then saturated at a level set by arrival order (−15, −25, −33 in units of M for inputs that arrived after both, between, before the top two).
4. 'One per earlier input' overstates independence: overwriting one old input moves the entry of the next-newer position by 0.4 of its own change; the tape needs 5 principal components for 95 % of its variance at t = 9; within a sequence Spearman(score, value) over the 8 non-top-2 inputs is 0.32.
5. T3 is not evidence for a specific mechanism. h_t(x_0..x_t) = h_9(0,…,0,x_0..x_t) exactly, so T3 is the network's accuracy on zero-prefixed inputs. In 672 independently trained bias-free networks of the same architecture, 354 of the 379 with accuracy ≥ 0.90 show the same early readout, and 292 of 379 have a shift cosine ≥ 0.9 (M_16,10 is among the 7 cleanest). The property also fails without a positive runner-up: 0.22–0.59 for S_t ≤ 0, at chance when M_t ≤ 0.

## Objections tested

- **T3 is trivial in the sense that it follows from the architecture plus the trained function, so it cannot count as evidence for a tape mechanism in particular.** (affects conclusion: partly)
  - test: (a) Compared h_t(x_0..x_t) with h_9 of the same inputs behind 9 − t zeros (2000 sequences, t = 1..8). (b) Replaced the zeros by N(0, 0.01²) values and ran the full length-10 network (seed 5905, S > 0.05). (c) Ran the same early readout on the 672 bias-free networks of the replica sweep (same architecture and task, other seeds; read-only), 20k N(0,1) sequences, seed 6201.
  - result: (a) Identity is exact: largest difference 0.0. (b) Tiny-prefix accuracy, argmax over the last t+1 positions, against the early readout: 0.9916 / 0.9912 (t=1), 0.9888 / 0.9884 (t=2), 0.9766 / 0.9761 (t=3), 0.9723 / 0.9720 (t=5), 0.9708 / 0.9709 (t=7). ## Bugs or issues

- No coding bug found in e10_tape_shift.py, e12_early_readout.py, e13_provisional_scores.py (read only after all my numbers were produced). Definitions match the claims file; their seeds are 1010 (100k), 1212 (400k), 1313 (400k).
- T1: the single figure 0.095 mixes two very different things. Rows 0–8 have relative residual 0.027; row 9 has 0.705 and carries 92 % of the squared residual. Report them separately.
- T1: the row-9 entries 1.22–1.62 are a conditioning effect of pinv(U_o). They multiply the sum of all logits, which the readout barely emits (smallest singular value 0.293, left vector = all-ones over positions, cos 0.997). With that direction dropped, row 9 is at most 0.39 in magnitude. Do not read row 9 as a rule for the newest score.

The resting gate pattern occurs in 5–29 % of real states, the ordinary part carries 13–92 % of the real tape's within-state variance (t = 1..9), and U_o A h[ORD] predicts the real next scores of entries aged ≥ 2 with R² −2.9, −1.8, −0.4, 0.56, 0.75, 0.81, 0.86 (t = 3..9). - T4, age 0: 61.6 is a through-origin cross-sectional slope. The derivative at fixed history is 61.9 (sd 0.36) and d = 62.0 v + 0.44; the tie point is at v = −0.013 (median), so the newest input already outscores the runner-up in half the cases when it is within 2 % of M below it (P(d &lt; 0) = 0.49 for v in (−0.02, 0], 0.857 for (−0.05, −0.02]).
